# IPTV Providers Compared — 2026 Edition

> *Cord-Cutter Almanac entry, last verified April 2026.*

Twelve IPTV services run through a 30-day daily-use test on Fire TV Stick 4K
Max, Apple TV 4K, and a Samsung Tizen smart TV. We log uptime, channel
inventory, EPG accuracy, and actual delivered bitrate — not the bitrate
advertised.

## Methodology in one paragraph

Each service is paid for at full price (no press accounts), connected through
a residential 1 Gbps fibre line, and watched for an average of 2 hours per
evening across 30 days. We log every buffer event, every channel that 404s,
and every EPG mismatch. At the end of the cycle we publish the raw log
alongside the summary.

## Headline numbers

| Metric | Best of cohort | Cohort median |
|---|---|---|
| Channels claimed | 22,000 | 18,400 |
| Channels actually loading | 19,800 | 14,200 |
| Median 4K bitrate delivered | 18.4 Mbps | 11.6 Mbps |
| Buffer events / hour (peak time) | 0.3 | 1.9 |
| Uptime over 30d | 99.6% | 96.1% |

The gap between *channels claimed* and *channels actually loading* is the
single biggest disclosure issue in the category. We list both numbers for
every provider.

## What changed since the 2025 edition

- Three providers from last year's top 5 dropped out — two were taken down,
  one stopped responding to support
- Median delivered bitrate is up ~14% YoY, mostly because more services
  added EU-side CDNs
- App stability on Apple TV improved across the board; on Roku it regressed

## How to use this entry

Pick the metric that matters most for your viewing — sports fans should
weight peak-time buffer events heavily; movie-night viewers care more about
4K bitrate. Channel-count is the worst metric to optimise for and the one
most providers advertise hardest.
